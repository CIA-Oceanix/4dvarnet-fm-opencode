\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Operational data assimilation (DA) is built on commitments so standard that they
are seldom stated as hypotheses at all. The analysis is produced by cycling:
propagate a state estimate, update it when observations arrive, repeat. Skill is
understood to follow model fidelity, so model development and DA development are
the same programme. The state is a point in the phase space of a differential
equation, and its uncertainty is a perturbation of that point. Prior errors
travel by the same integration that carries the signal.

Each of these buys something real, and none is foolish. Together, we argue, they
also explain two symptoms the field observes repeatedly and tends to treat as
tuning problems:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em,style=nextline]
  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{S-a --- the marginal value of observations.}}]
    DA converts additional sparse observations into skill inefficiently, and the
    inefficiency worsens sharply under model error. The quantity of interest is
    not RMSE at a given density but
    $\mathrm{d}\,\text{skill}/\mathrm{d}\,\text{density}$, at matched everything.
  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{S-b --- posterior quality.}}]
    DA's posterior characterisation is structurally impoverished, not merely
    mis-calibrated: the deficit is a property of the estimator class, and no
    inflation parameter repairs it.
\end{description}

\paragraph{What this paper is, and is not.}
This is a \emph{diagnosis} paper. We propose no new assimilation scheme. That is
deliberate rather than a limitation of scope: a method paper invites the reply
``your baseline was under-tuned'', whereas the claim here concerns what the
standard baselines cannot do \emph{however well tuned}. We therefore report the
tuning-sensitivity studies (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}) as evidence that the
baselines were mapped rather than assumed --- including one that returned a
negative result --- and we include weak-constraint 4D-Var, the classical
formulation explicitly permitted to know that the model is wrong, so that the
classical family is represented by its best member rather than its most
convenient one.

\paragraph{Conditional flow matching as the instrument.}
The difficulty with ``method A beats method B'' comparisons is that they do not
say \emph{what} was missing. Section~\ref{sec:methods} adopts a conditional-flow
representation of the posterior, in which every scheme compared here --- 4D-Var,
ensemble filters, a supervised regression network, a conditional flow, a
score-based sampler, and blends of these --- is a special case of one operator
$\Psiop = \Psimean + \PsiG + \PsiNG$. The classical schemes do not merely score
worse: they \emph{identically zero} named terms of that operator. This converts a
benchmark into an attribution, and it is what lets each measured deficit be tied
to a named hypothesis. We use the representation as a measuring instrument, not
as a proposed estimator.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The inference target is the \textbf{state} throughout. Joint state--parameter
estimation is a different problem with a different cost and is out of scope;
where model parameters appear they are inputs an estimator may condition on,
never quantities it estimates. Unrolled variational solvers are likewise
excluded: they form a fourth operator family requiring gradient machinery this
paper does not introduce.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item Four structural hypotheses (\textbf{H1}--\textbf{H3b},
    Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}) stated so they can be falsified, each relaxable
    \emph{separately} so the resulting change can be attributed.
  \item A diagnostic use of the conditional-flow partition
    (Section~\ref{sec:methods}) that states exactly which posterior components
    each method class can represent, and which it zeroes by construction.
  \item The measurement that model error \emph{collapses the marginal value of
    observations} rather than adding a constant offset
    (Section~\ref{sec:marginal}): a $6.2\times$ collapse for strong-constraint
    4D-Var against $1.9\times$ for ensemble filters. To the best of our survey
    this effect is unreported.
  \item The result that the model's sensitivity to parameters is \emph{not} a
    proxy for the posterior's sensitivity to them
    (Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}), with the methodological consequence that
    pricing parameter uncertainty by forward propagation overstates its cost
    whenever the observations are informative.
  \item The result that the choice of state variable, at bit-identical physics,
    changes analysis skill substantially and predictably
    (Section~\ref{sec:representation}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Testbeds.}
Three systems, each with a distinct job: a stochastic Lorenz-63 for the
model-error axis including weak-constraint 4D-Var; a two-scale Lorenz-96 as the
main testbed, the only one carrying all five scheme classes; and a two-layer
quasi-geostrophic channel for representation and realism
(Section~\ref{sec:testbeds}). All are idealised --- the price of being able to
define ``matched everything'' precisely enough for a marginal-value measurement
to mean something. We return to transfer in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
