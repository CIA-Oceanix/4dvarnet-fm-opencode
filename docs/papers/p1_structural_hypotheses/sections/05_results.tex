\textbf{Occupying more operator slots does not by itself buy accuracy.} SDA1
fills all three and is the \emph{weakest} learned scheme here (0.5063);
DirectUNet fills $\Psimean$ alone and beats it (0.4699); the conditional flows
fill all three and beat both (0.3412--0.3536). The slots determine what a scheme
\emph{can represent}, not how well it estimates --- and
Section~\ref{sec:nongaussian} shows the same non-monotonicity in the dispersion
column, in the opposite direction.

Two observations set up everything that follows. First, the degradation is not a
uniform error floor: it varies from $1.68\times$ to $2.74\times$ across systems
and, on QG's potential-vorticity field, turns a positive skill score negative.
Second, the schemes that do not degrade are precisely those whose prior is not an
integrated model. The rest of this section asks \emph{why}, and attributes each
part of the answer to a named hypothesis.

\subsection{Conditioning on model information is nearly inert}
\label{sec:inert}

If the learned schemes are robust to \So{}, the obvious question is whether they
use $\thv^{*}$ at all.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{scheme} & \textbf{conditioning} & \textbf{\Sz{}} & \textbf{\So{}} & $\So/\Sz$ & \textbf{vs.\ SDA1} \\
\midrule
SDA1-M & none (unconditional prior) & \textbf{0.5063} & 0.5049 & 0.997 & --- \\
SDA2-M & \emph{true} $\thv$ $+$ corrupted $\zv^{*}$ & 0.5129 & 0.5130 & 1.000 & $+1.3\%$ \\
SDA3-M & \emph{noisy} $\thv^{*}$ & 0.5228 & 0.5229 & 1.000 & $+3.3\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Lorenz-96 conditioning ladder at the P1 protocol, M tier, pooled RMSE.
\textbf{Both forms of model-information conditioning are worse than none.}
Source: \texttt{reports/l96/outputs/p1\_l96\_benchmark.md}.}
\label{tab:ladder}
\end{table}

Conditioning on model information moves \Sz{} skill by at most a few percent,
and it does not help: relative to the unconditional prior, true parameters cost
$1.3\%$ and noisy ones $3.3\%$. Conditioning is, to a good approximation,
\emph{inert} --- and to the extent it does anything, it is mildly harmful.

\caveat{An earlier evaluation of the same ladder, at a different protocol,
reported the opposite sign for the noisy variant (SDA3 $3.0\%$ \emph{better}
than SDA1, against $3.3\%$ worse here). That the sign of a ${\sim}3\%$ effect
flips between protocols is itself the finding: the effect is smaller than the
checkpoint-selection noise the benchmark measures at $15$--$21\%$ for this
family. The inertness conclusion is robust; any claim about the \emph{direction}
of the conditioning effect is not, and the draft no longer makes one.}

This is the sharpest available statement of the thesis. The learned schemes are
robust to corrupted model parameters largely \emph{because they barely depend on
them} --- the observations carry the information. Classical DA has no such
fallback. H2 binds not because model information is unhelpful, but because DA
cannot down-weight it.

\paragraph{The training-exposure control has been run.}
The configuration trained with zero forcing/state bias --- the only one never
exposed to \So{}-level error in training --- gives \Sz{} 0.7046 / \So{} 0.7033,
ratio 0.998, indistinguishable from its exposed sibling (0.997).
\caveat{This control has no counterpart in the canonical benchmark table, which
is scoped to one backbone family; it is cited to its own run artefact.}

\paragraph{QG separates the definitional case from the real one.}
The QG DirectUNet ladder makes Table~\ref{tab:classes}'s caveat concrete. Q1 is
obs-only, so its \So{} numbers are \emph{bit-identical} to its \Sz{} numbers
($\psi$ EV 0.9805 both) --- definitional, and carrying no robustness
information. Q2 is oracle-conditioned and is the best scheme at \Sz{} (0.9827)
but falls to 0.9490 under \So{}. Q3 and Q4, conditioned on \emph{noisy} model
information, hold (0.9798 $\to$ 0.9763; 0.9824 $\to$ 0.9808). Conditioning on
\emph{true} model information is again the fragile choice, exactly as on L96.

\paragraph{Consequence: ODE sensitivity is not posterior sensitivity.}
\label{sec:sensitivity}
Two distinct sensitivities are in play, and classical DA cannot separate them:
the \emph{model} sensitivity $\partial \xv_{\mathrm{ODE}}/\partial(\thv,\zv)$ of
the free-running trajectory, Lyapunov-amplified and large; and the
\emph{posterior} sensitivity $\partial\, p(\xv_1\mid\Cv)/\partial(\thv,\zv)$ at
fixed $\yv$. Classical DA conflates them \textbf{by construction}, because its
estimator \emph{is} the model: by~\eqref{eq:ssm} every route from $(\thv,\zv)$ to
the analysis passes through $\Phi$. Its \Sz{}$\to$\So{} degradation therefore
measures \emph{its own estimator's} sensitivity, and cannot be read as a
measurement of how much $(\thv,\zv)$ uncertainty matters for the posterior.

The ladder above measures the other quantity directly: a conditioned amortised
estimator takes $(\thv^{*},\zv^{*})$ as inputs at fixed $\yv$, so perturbing the
conditioning while holding the observations fixed is a finite-difference estimate
of posterior sensitivity. It moves skill by $\leq 1.5\%$, and full corruption by
$\sim 0.2\%$, against the $1.68$--$1.80\times$ by which the classical schemes
degrade over the same perturbation.

\textbf{Assessing parameter and forcing uncertainty by propagating it through the
model therefore overstates its effect on the posterior whenever the observations
are informative}, and the overstatement is exactly the gap between the two
sensitivities. This is an inference from the two measurements above rather than a
separate experiment; a direct measurement would sweep both sensitivities over one
grid of observation density $\times$ perturbation amplitude, and would also test
the prediction that posterior sensitivity \emph{grows} as observations sparsify,
since less of $(\thv,\zv)$ is then screened off by $\yv$.
\needsrun{Both sensitivities on one density $\times$ amplitude grid. The
crossover density would be the paper's second headline number.}

Under model error, doubling the observations buys strong-constraint 4D-Var
3.2\%. In the fast-variable subspace, explained variance goes
$+0.417 \to -0.011$ at the sparser density and $+0.622 \to +0.047$ at the denser
one.

\textbf{H2 does not merely set an error floor; it decouples the analysis from the
observations.} A method whose prior is a wrong model cannot be rescued by more
data --- exactly the regime of sparse-observation geophysical DA. This is also
H3b's shadow: at low density DA has nothing but the model, so model error and
observation sparsity \emph{compound rather than add}.

That compounding is the more general pattern, and it recurs on the other
structural axis: Section~\ref{sec:representation} shows model error and the
choice of state representation compounding in the same way, the cost of a
representation swap growing by an order of magnitude between \Sz{} and \So{}.
Model error is not one deficit among four --- it is the multiplier on the
others.

\paragraph{Weak-constraint 4D-Var bounds this claim.}
\label{sec:weak4dvar}
Weak-constraint 4D-Var frees a per-step model-error term and is therefore the one
classical scheme \emph{permitted} to know the model is wrong --- the honest
ceiling for the family. It has been run on two of the three systems, and on both
it is the strongest classical scheme under model error: on L63 it beats
strong-constraint by 22\% at \So{} (1.633 against 2.100), and on QG it leads on
both fields at \So{} ($\psi$ 0.9468 against 0.9314; $q$ $-0.501$ against
$-0.857$). The gap in Table~\ref{tab:overview-gap} is therefore not an artefact
of comparing against a strawman: the classical family is represented by its best
member.

It also bounds what may be claimed here. \textbf{The collapse in
Table~\ref{tab:marginal} is established for strong-constraint and filtering
methods only}; weak-constraint 4D-Var is the formulation that explicitly relaxes
H2, and the corresponding measurement on the density grid does not exist --- L96
is the one system where it has not been run at all.
\needsrun{Weak-constraint 4D-Var on Lorenz-96 across the observation-density
grid. The implementation exists; the benchmark run does not. Single
highest-value outstanding experiment, because it is the only one that can widen
or narrow the paper's headline claim.}

\subsection{The representation decides which fields are reconstructed, and model
error widens the trade}
\label{sec:representation}

The cleanest manipulation available in this study. The QG \texttt{psi\_state}
variant integrates the \emph{same} dynamics with the streamfunction as the state
variable instead of potential vorticity. The $q$-space physics is bit-identical
--- free forecasts agree to $\sim 10^{-6}$ relative --- so nothing changes except
which variable is called ``the state'', and with it the observation operator,
which becomes a trivial index lookup.

\addlinespace
\multirow{2}{*}{\So{} (model error)} & $q$-state (default) & $+0.328$ & $\mathbf{+0.428}$ & $+0.304$ \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\label{tab:representation}

The mechanism is the map between the two representations: $q \approx \nabla^2\psi$
amplifies high-wavenumber $\psi$-analysis error by $K^2$, so a scheme that fits
$\psi$ well can be arbitrarily bad in $q$ while remaining, by its own metric,
excellent.

\textbf{The size of the trade is the result, not its sign.} At \Sz{} the swap
buys $+0.009$ on $\psi$ and costs $-0.169$ on $q$. At \So{} it buys $+0.278$ and
costs $-3.353$. The representation choice is nearly free when the model is
correct and catastrophic when it is not, which places this result on the paper's
main axis rather than beside it: H3a and H2 are not independent, because the
error the representation amplifies is exactly the error model discrepancy
creates.

\paragraph{The same effect across the classical schemes.}
This is not confined to a single controlled swap. In
Table~\ref{tab:overview-robust} the variational schemes --- which optimise a
$\psi$-observation cost --- carry \emph{negative} $q$ scores at \Sz{}
($-0.126$, $-0.034$) falling to $-0.857$ and $-0.501$ at \So{}, while the
ensemble filters, updating in $q$, hold near $+0.4$. The learned schemes, trained
to reconstruct the target field directly rather than inheriting it through an
inversion, are best of all on $q$ ($0.60$--$0.67$). Which field a scheme
reconstructs well tracks the representation it works in, not how much information
the observations carry --- the observations are identical across every row.

\paragraph{What is missing: the same manipulation on the learned side.}
The controlled evidence here is all on the DA side, where the state variable is
an explicit modelling choice. The learned schemes have a representation too ---
the space their inputs and outputs are expressed in --- but no equivalent
controlled swap has been run.
\caveat{An existing L96 per-channel normalisation ablation reports that
retraining in a normalised space \emph{degrades} skill substantially (explained
variance $0.857 \to 0.027$). That result is not used here: it was measured on a
different backbone from the schemes in this paper, whose best configurations
\emph{do} normalise, so it cannot be reconciled without a matched rerun.}
The experiment is well defined, because the existing QG network is already one
arm of it. Its supervision target is the two-layer streamfunction, and $q$ is
obtained by spectrally inverting the predicted $\psi$ --- structurally the same
path as the DA $\psi$-state variant, and subject to the same $K^2$
amplification. What is missing is the $q$-output arm. One complication makes the
design a $2\times 2$ rather than a straight swap: the network carries an optional
auxiliary $q$-loss (weight $1/\mathrm{Var}(q)$), which supervises the inverted
field directly and so partially decouples \emph{output space} from \emph{what is
supervised}. Both must be varied to attribute the effect to either.

\textbf{The prediction, stated in advance.} If the effect is a property of
inverse problems rather than of DA specifically, the $q$-output arm should beat
the $\psi$-output arm on $q$ and lose to it on $\psi$, and the gap should widen
between \Sz{} and \So{} as it does in Table~\ref{tab:representation}. If instead
the auxiliary loss accounts for most of the difference --- that is, if
$\psi$-output \emph{with} a $q$-loss matches $q$-output --- then the operative
choice is what is \emph{supervised}, not what is \emph{represented}, and H3a is a
weaker hypothesis than the DA result suggests. The existing numbers already hint
at the second reading: the $\psi$-output network reaches $q$ EV $0.62$--$0.67$
where the DA $\psi$-state variant collapses to $0.583$ and then $-2.925$, and the
one structural difference between them is that $q$-loss.

A spread/skill column is included because the swap should also be read against
Section~\ref{sec:nongaussian}: if changing the output space moves calibration as
well as accuracy, the representation is not separable from the dispersion
question either.

The accuracy/dispersion trade is real and stark --- the best-calibrated schemes
are the least accurate and vice versa, with no scheme good at both --- but it
does not run in the direction the operator partition suggests. The class that
zeroes $\PsiNG$ is the better-calibrated one.

\paragraph{What this does and does not license.}
It does not rescue the ensemble-Kalman class: at \Sz{} it is $2.5\times$ less
accurate than a conditional flow, and under model error it degrades by
$1.7\times$ while the flows do not
(Table~\ref{tab:overview-robust}). What it does is refute a specific inference
--- that a missing $\PsiNG$ must show up as a dispersion deficit.

The reconciliation is that \textbf{spread/RMSE is a second-moment diagnostic, and
$\PsiNG$ is not a second-moment object.} A Gaussian update can match the
posterior's spread exactly and still be wrong about its \emph{shape}: skewness,
tail weight, multimodality. Nothing in Table~\ref{tab:calibration} can see that.
The diagnostic that can --- the rank histogram, which tests the full predictive
distribution rather than its variance --- \textbf{does not exist anywhere in the
codebase}.

So the honest position is that C4 as originally stated is not supported by the
evidence available, and the evidence that would decide it has not been produced.
We state it that way rather than leaning on the construction argument alone.
\needsrun{Rank histograms across all classes at a uniform $N=30$. This is the
single experiment that would settle C4 in either direction, and it is the
paper's largest new-code item.}

\paragraph{Blending, on a separate protocol.}
A sweep over the deterministic/generative blend measured the trade within one
family: mean-only gives RMSE $0.4934$ at spread/RMSE $0.000$; the sampler alone
$0.5686$ at $1.202$; a hard warm start $0.4268$ at $0.284$; and a continuous
blend $(\lambda_0 = 0.5, p = 1)$ $\mathbf{0.4202}$ at $\mathbf{0.380}$ ---
dominating the warm start on \emph{both} axes, the one place the trade is not
adversarial.
\caveat{These come from the sampler study's own configuration
(variance-weighted guidance, $N_\mathrm{outer} = 50$) and are not comparable with
Table~\ref{tab:calibration}, which is the P1 protocol. The blended schemes were
not re-run under P1, so blending is reported as a within-family result only.}

\subsection{A motivating ordering that is not yet evidence}
\label{sec:ordering}

At \Sz{} on Lorenz-96 the schemes order by how much of the window they treat at
once: sequential filters (ETKF 0.8662, EnKF 0.8943) are worse than the window
variational method (Strong-4D-Var 0.7383), which is in turn far worse than the
window-amortised learned schemes (0.3412--0.5063). Read against
Table~\ref{tab:zeroing}, the ordering is \emph{not} monotone in the number of
operator slots occupied --- EnKF fills $\PsiG$ and still trails a
$\Psimean$-only variational scheme --- which is itself informative: how much of
the problem is solved \emph{jointly} matters here more than which posterior
components are representable.

This is suggestive of H1 but \textbf{confounded}: amortisation, learning and
capacity all vary at once, and the learned schemes see a training set the
classical ones do not.
\needsrun{Filter vs.\ window-smoother vs.\ window-amortised at matched model,
matched observations and matched compute.}
