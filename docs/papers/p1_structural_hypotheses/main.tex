\begin{abstract}
Model-based data assimilation (DA) often loses to estimators that use less
physics: observation-only products against ocean reanalyses, learned priors
against the true dynamical model, neural schemes against classical ones. We
treat every DA scheme, classical or learned, as an estimator of the conditional
distribution of the state given its context, and decompose its error along a
chain of idealisations: an irreducible term, a \emph{restriction} (the scheme
uses less information than is available), a \emph{misspecification} (it
believes a wrong model) and an \emph{approximation} (it computes its own target
only approximately). We extend this chain from the mean to the reported
uncertainty with a flow-matching score, $\FMS_\tau$, which equals the
mean-squared error at $\tau = 0$ and is strictly proper for $\tau \in (0, 1)$;
the split into irreducible, restriction and the remainder is exact at every
$\tau$. On a two-scale Lorenz-96 system with a perfect model and with a biased
one, under matched information and tuning parity, we find that \todo{one
number per question: Q1 smoothing and ensemble size remove most of the
perfect-model DA deficit; Q2 model error shows in the large-$\tau$ score
(ETKS calibration loss 34\,\% $\to$ 63\,\%) and collapses the marginal value of
observations of strong-constraint 4D-Var $7\times$; Q3 a learned prior beats the
30-member smoother but not the 100-member one}. A restriction costs variance
honestly and preserves calibration; a misspecification costs bias silently and
destroys it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The puzzle.}
\todo{GLORYS vs DUACS on sea-surface height (effective resolution 239 vs 151 km,
Gulf Stream example); a learned prior beating DA with the true ODE
\cite{fablet4dvarnet}; neural-DA skill claims
\cite{brajard2020combining,bocquet2020bayesian,rozet2023sda}.} Three candidate
explanations are rarely separated: the dynamical model is wrong; the algorithm
(filtering, Gaussian updates, finite ensembles, local optimisation) loses
information; or the scheme does not use all the information available.

\figplaceholder{Fig.~1 -- the puzzle}{(a) SSH: observation-only OI vs model-based reanalysis; (b) learned prior vs true-ODE 4D-Var (JAMES 2020).}

\paragraph{The idea.}
Every DA scheme estimates the conditional expectation $\E[\xv \mid C]$ and,
ideally, the conditional distribution $p(\xv \mid C)$. Its error splits along a
chain of idealisations (Sect.~\ref{sec:chain}). Two schemes with the same RMSE
can have different budgets, and only the budget says what to fix. The same chain
extends to the uncertainty a scheme reports, with a single proper score that
reduces to the mean-squared error at $\tau = 0$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:fms}).

\paragraph{Questions.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Q\arabic*},leftmargin=*]
  \item With a perfect model, how close does each scheme get to $\E[\xv\mid C]$, and where does the rest of its error go?
  \item What does model error do to the budget, and to the uncertainty each scheme reports?
  \item Does the form of the prior --- derived from the model equations or learned from data --- drive performance?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) The error chain applied to classical and learned DA under matched
information, with each term estimated by a controlled contrast. (ii) Its
extension along $\tau$ with $\FMS_\tau$, with an exact split at every $\tau$.
(iii) A controlled two-scale Lorenz-96 study: perfect model and model error, two
observing systems, five DA and five learned schemes, every inference
hyper-parameter tuned on validation windows.

\paragraph{What this paper is not.}
It proposes no new DA method; the target is the state only; unrolled
variational solvers are out of scope \todo{P2}; the general flow-matching
representation of DA algorithms is left to future work \todo{ML paper}.

\section{DA as estimation of a conditional distribution}\label{sec:chain}

\subsection{State-space model and context}
\todo{port eqs.\ dynamics / obs / ssm / context from main.old.tex, Sect.~2.1.}
The state trajectory $\xv$ over a window follows two-scale dynamics with
per-window parameters $\thv$ and stochastic forcing $\zv$; observations are
$\yv_k = H\xv_k + \epsilon_k$, $\epsilon_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R)$. With the
context $C = (\yv, \thv, \zv)$,
\begin{equation}
  p(\xv \mid C) \;\propto\; p(\yv \mid \xv)\; p(\xv \mid \thv, \zv).
\end{equation}
$\E[\xv\mid C]$ is the MSE-optimal estimator and $\E\tr\Cov(\xv\mid C)$ the
irreducible error.

\subsection{The error chain}
A scheme uses the information $S \subseteq C$, believes a model $\tilde p$ and
computes $\hat m$:
\begin{equation}
  m = \E[\xv\mid C] \;\to\; m_S = \E[\xv\mid S] \;\to\; \tilde m = \E_{\tilde p}[\xv\mid S] \;\to\; \hat m .
\end{equation}
If $\hat m$ depends only on $S$ and on randomness independent of the truth,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain0}
  \mathrm{MSE}(\hat m) \;=\; \underbrace{\E\tr\Cov(\xv\mid C)}_{\text{irreducible}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\E\|m - m_S\|^2}_{\text{restriction}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\E\|m_S - \hat m\|^2}_{\text{misspecification + approximation}},
\end{equation}
both splits being exact by the tower property. The last term contains the
misspecification $\E\|m_S - \tilde m\|^2$, the approximation
$\E\|\tilde m - \hat m\|^2$ and a cross term; we separate them by controlled
contrasts (Sect.~\ref{sec:design}), not by an identity.

\section{Scoring the uncertainty: the error chain along $\tau$}\label{sec:fms}

\subsection{The operator of a scheme}
On the path $\xv_\tau = \tau\xv_1 + (1-\tau)\xv_0$ with $\xv_0 \sim
\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ (z-scored units), any output distribution $q$ of a scheme
defines the operator
\begin{equation}
  \Psi_q(\xv_\tau, \tau) \;=\; \E_q[\xv_1 \mid \xv_\tau] \;=\; \Psi_{\mathrm{mean}} + \Psi_{\mathrm{anom}} .
\end{equation}
\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\caption{The operator $\Psi_q$ of each class of scheme ($\snr = \tau^2/(1-\tau)^2$).}
\label{tab:psi}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
scheme & $q$ & $\Psi_q(\xv_\tau,\tau)$ \\
\midrule
Strong/Weak-4DVar, DirectUNet & point mass at $\hat m$ & $\hat m$ \quad ($\Psi_{\mathrm{anom}} \equiv 0$) \\
EnKF, ETKF, ETKS (Gaussian form) & $\mathcal{N}(m, v)$ & $m + \dfrac{\snr\, v}{1 + \snr\, v}\,(\xv_\tau/\tau - m)$ \\
CFM, SDA, ensemble KDE & samples & posterior-weighted average of samples \\
hybrid DirectUNet $\to$ SDA3-fix & composition & $\Psi_{\mathrm{mean}}$ from DirectUNet, $\Psi_{\mathrm{anom}}$ from SDA \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
Conditional flow matching learns $\Psi$ directly; the general representation of
DA algorithms as flows is beyond this paper.

\subsection{The score}
\begin{equation}
  \FMS_\tau(q) \;=\; \E\big\|\Psi_q(\xv_\tau, \tau) - \xv_1^\star\big\|^2 .
\end{equation}
Properties (proofs in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}): (i) $\FMS_0$ is the MSE of the
mean; (ii) $\FMS_\tau$ is strictly proper for $\tau \in (0,1)$; (iii) for a
Gaussian it is minimised at spread$^2$ = MSE at every $\tau$; (iv) as $\tau \to
1$ it tends to a universal floor plus the Hyvärinen score; (v) integrated with
weight $d\snr$ it gives the log score up to a constant \todo{cite Verdú 2010};
(vi) a point estimate scores its MSE at every $\tau$.

\subsection{The chain at every $\tau$}
Let $\Psi_p$ and $\Psi_{p_S}$ be the true operators given $C$ and $S$. Under the
condition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:chain0},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chaintau}
  \FMS_\tau(q) \;=\; \underbrace{\mathrm{mmse}_p(\tau)}_{\text{irreducible}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\E\|\Psi_p - \Psi_{p_S}\|^2}_{R_\tau(S)\text{: restriction}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\E\|\Psi_q - \Psi_{p_S}\|^2}_{D_\tau(q\|p_S)\text{: misspec.\ + approx.}},
\end{equation}
exact at every $\tau$ (tower property conditioning on $(\xv_\tau, S)$), with
$D_\tau \ge 0$ and $D_\tau = 0$ iff $q = p_S$. Consequences:
differences between schemes are exact operator divergences, the unknown
$\mathrm{mmse}_p(\tau)$ being common to all; the filter/smoother identity holds
along $\tau$ for exact schemes; and a restriction raises the $\FMS_\tau$ curve
while a misspecification bends it upward at large $\tau$.

\subsection{Derived diagnostics}
\emph{Calibration loss}: the share of $\FMS_\tau$ removed by the best scalar
variance rescaling $c\,v$, with optimum $c^\star(\tau)$. \emph{Shape gain}: KDE vs
Gaussian at the same variance. \emph{Pooled spread/skill}
$\sqrt{\E v / \E e^2}$. \caveat{the per-window ratio of mean spread to mean RMSE
is biased low and is not used.}

\paragraph{Relation to CRPS and the log score.}
The chain decomposition is not unique to $\FMS$: the expected log score splits
the same way through Kullback--Leibler divergences. $\FMS_\tau$ is used because
it is computable for samples, Gaussians \emph{and} point estimates (the log score
is not), is resolved over $\tau$, separating mean and uncertainty, and equals the
MSE at $\tau = 0$. \todo{cite CRPS / energy score / Bröcker 2009 decomposition.}

\section{Experimental design}\label{sec:design}

\paragraph{System and cases.}
Two-scale Lorenz-96 (8 slow + 32 fast variables, $dt = 0.001$, 3000-step
windows), per-window $\thv$ ($\pm$20\,\%) and stochastic forcing $\zv$; 24
observed channels, $R = 0.5$. \Sz: the assimilating model is the truth.
\So: the model is told biased $\thv_{\mathrm{da}}$ (+10\,\%) and a corrupted
forcing; the truth is unchanged. \needsrun{$\delta$ sweep: $\delta \in \{0, 5,
10, 20, 40\,\%\}$ crossed with stated $\sigma \in \{0, 20\,\%\}$, plus one
structural error.}

\paragraph{Observing systems.}
The regular 30-obs grid and the canonical random layout (10--100 observations at
stratified times, 4--16 fast channels, the exact observations the DA schemes
assimilate); an $n_{\mathrm{obs}} \times k$ factorial and out-of-range probes.

\paragraph{Schemes.}
DA: ETKF, EnKF, ETKS (30 members; 100 as a sensitivity), Strong-4DVar,
Weak-4DVar. Learned: DirectUNet, VanillaCFM, PredictStateCFM, SDA1 / SDA2 /
SDA3-fix, and the DirectUNet $\to$ SDA3-fix hybrid; trained on a random
observing system with fresh noise every batch, 1200 epochs, 3 seeds.
\todo{Table 2: schemes, design-matrix labels (F1--U-model, Appendix~\ref{app:matrix}), tuned hyper-parameters.}

\paragraph{Matched information.}
Every main arm sees the same $\yv$; model-based arms get the \Sz{} or \So{}
model. Learned arms trained on the true system are a \emph{reference bound} at
\So{} (the oracle confound). \needsrun{M-trained learned arms, $\sigma = 0$ and
20\,\%.}

\paragraph{Tuning parity.}
Every inference hyper-parameter is tuned on 50 validation windows: inflation per
case, ETKS lag and retro-inflation, Weak-4DVar model-error scale and iterations,
SDA guidance weight, hybrid $\tau_0$ and guidance (Appendix~\ref{app:tuning}).

\paragraph{Estimating the terms.}
Irreducible: upper-bounded by the best scheme. Restriction: the filter/smoother
identity on one ensemble; conditioning contrasts ($\thv$, $\zv$).
Approximation: ensemble size (30 vs 100), L-BFGS iterations (10 vs 40),
training budget and data. Misspecification: \Sz{} $\to$ \So{} at fixed scheme,
the $\delta$ sweep and the model-uncertainty arms.

\paragraph{Scoring.}
RMSE, pooled spread/skill and $\FMS_\tau$ at $\tau \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75\}$
plus the uniform-$d\tau$ integral; Gaussian form for every scheme, KDE for the
sample-based ones; physical units; 95\,\% window-bootstrap intervals and paired
tests.

\section{Q2 --- what model error does to the budget}\label{sec:q2}

\subsection{The budget at $\tau = 0$}
Under model error the DA errors grow about sevenfold in MSE (ETKS 0.497 $\to$
1.338); 100 members change nothing (1.338 $\to$ 1.338); smoothing gains only
5\,\% and the filter/smoother identity fails (MSE gap 0.237, variance gap 0.534,
mean shift 0.913): misspecification dominates. The learned rows are flat
(DirectUNet 0.339, hybrid 0.289), as an oracle bound.

\subsection{The misspecification signature along $\tau$}
DA calibration loss at $\tau = 0.75$ grows from \Sz{} to \So{} (ETKS 34 $\to$
63\,\%, $c^\star \ge 8$; ETKF 14 $\to$ 23\,\% regular, 3 $\to$ 14\,\% random), and
the DA ensembles become short-tailed (+17--30\,\% for KDE vs Gaussian). The
learned rows are invariant, except SDA2, whose loss falls. Aggregate
calibration is not calibration: on random \So{} the ETKF's pooled spread/skill
is 0.99, yet it loses 12\,\% at $\tau = 0.75$.

\section{Q3 --- does the form of the prior drive performance?}\label{sec:q3}

\subsection{Same likelihood, two priors}
The learned joint-window prior (SDA, 0.453--0.464) beats the true-ODE hard
constraint (Strong-4DVar, 0.703) and the 30-member smoother (0.497), but not the
100-member smoother (0.393): the ordering is set by the approximation term, not
by the fidelity of the prior. Along $\tau$ the SDA operator is better calibrated
than the smoother's (7--18\,\% vs 34\,\% loss).
\figplaceholder{Fig.~8}{Model-derived vs learned priors: RMSE and $\FMS_\tau$.}

\subsection{A better prior is not a better estimate}
Training the SDA prior 400 $\to$ 1200 epochs gains 7--9\,\%; the true ODE used as
a hard prior (Strong-4DVar) is the worst DA row at \Sz.

\subsection{Conditioning the prior}
$\thv$ (SDA1 $\to$ SDA2 / SDA3-fix) and forcing $\zv$ as restriction terms; SDA2
at \So{} as the misspecification case. \needsrun{SDA $\pm$ forcing.}

\subsection{Composition}
The hybrid composes DirectUNet's mean with SDA3-fix's guided sampler. It has
the best mean everywhere (0.293 / 0.367, flat at \So) but not the best
distribution: the flows overtake it at $\tau = 0.75$ (0.0195 vs 0.0200 regular,
0.025 vs 0.029 random, z-units). The score separates the two components it
composes.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Lessons, as budget statements.}
(a) At a perfect model the DA deficit is restriction and approximation: honest
and reducible. (b) Under model error misspecification dominates, and shows in
the large-$\tau$ score before it shows in the RMSE. (c) A better prior does not
imply a better estimate; approximate priors can beat the true physics. (d)
Model-based DA can misreport the information value of its conditioning
variables.

\paragraph{What classical DA still gets right.}
The sparse-observation regime; observing-system transfer through explicit $H,
R$; calibration when its assumptions hold; no training set.

\paragraph{Back to the puzzle.}
\todo{What the budgets suggest for reanalysis vs observation-only products
(misspecification under model error) and for learned-prior skill (approximation
and restriction at a perfect model), stated as hypotheses for real systems.}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
$\mathrm{mmse}_p(\tau)$ is unknown, so only differences and an upper bound are
available; DA is scored in Gaussian form; one idealised system; the oracle
confound; the cross term between misspecification and approximation is only
bounded by contrasts.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
\todo{Model-error DA \cite{tremolet2006weak,carrassi2018da,farchi2021model};
unstable-subspace DA \cite{trevisan2004unstable,bocquet2017unstable};

\paragraph{Outlook.}
Flow matching as a generic representation of DA algorithms; self-supervised
adaptation of learned schemes; transfer to a quasi-geostrophic system.
