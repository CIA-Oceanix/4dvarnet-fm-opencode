\subsection{Lorenz-96}
\paragraph{Dynamics.}
The two-scale system has $K = 8$ slow variables $X_k$ and $J = 4$ fast variables
$Y_{j,k}$ per slow variable,
\begin{align*}
  \dot X_k &= X_{k-1}(X_{k+1} - X_{k-2}) - X_k + F - h \textstyle\sum_{j} w_j Y_{j,k} + c_1 W(t),\\
  \dot Y_{j,k} &= \big[Y_{j+1,k}(Y_{j-1,k} - Y_{j-2,k}) - Y_{j,k} + h_x X_k\big] / \epsilon ,
\end{align*}
with periodic indices, nominal values $F = 8$, $h = h_x = c_1 = 1$, $\epsilon = 0.1$ and
coupling weights $w = (1, 1, 0.1, 0.1)$, and a slow external forcing $W(t)$ (an AR(1)
process plus a sinusoid). Each window draws its own parameters: $F$, $c_1$, $h_x$,
$\epsilon$ and $w$ are drawn uniformly within $\pm 20\,\%$ of their nominal values
($h$ is fixed).
The system is integrated with a fourth-order Runge--Kutta scheme, $\Delta t = 0.001$,
over windows of 3000 steps.

\paragraph{Observations.}
The observed state has 24 variables: the 8 slow variables and the first two fast
variables of each slow variable; the 16 other fast variables are never observed. The
observation-error variance is $\mathbf{R} = 0.5\,I$. Two observing systems are used:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Regular:} all 24 variables observed at 30 equally spaced times per window
    (every 100 steps, first step included);
  \item \textbf{Random:} the same windows observed at 10 to 100 times at stratified
    random positions (first step always observed, at least one observation per DA
    sub-window), with 4 to 16 of the 16 observable fast variables observed per window.
\end{itemize}
Tables~\ref{tab:s0} and~\ref{tab:s1} and Figs.~\ref{fig:budget-s0}
and~\ref{fig:budget-s1} use the regular observing system; the random one is analysed in
Sect.~\ref{sec:q1-density}. All schemes assimilate exactly the same observations.

\paragraph{Data sets.}
The test set has 200 windows. The neural schemes are trained on 1000 windows and
validated on 100, with a random observing system redrawn at every batch (10 to 300
observation times, 4 to 16 observed fast variables) and fresh observation noise. The DA
hyper-parameters are selected on 50 separate validation windows.

\paragraph{Model error.}
\Sz{} and \So{} are described in Sect.~\ref{sec:testbeds}. At \So{}, the DA model has
its parameters biased by $+10\,\%$, receives a corrupted forcing and is truncated to the
24 observed variables; in the \So-full variant, it keeps the 40 variables.

\subsection{QG ocean}
\paragraph{Dynamics.}
The two-layer quasi-geostrophic ocean is solved on a $64 \times 64$ doubly periodic grid
over 1000\,km, with a 2\,h time step, $\beta = 1.5 \times 10^{-11}$\,m$^{-1}$\,s$^{-1}$
and a layer-depth ratio $\delta = 0.25$. Each window draws its deformation radius
(12.75--17.25\,km), upper-layer mean flow (0.0425--0.0575\,m\,s$^{-1}$) and bottom drag.
The ocean is forced by the wind-stress curl of a low-order chaotic atmosphere of coupled
gyrostats, projected on 12 Fourier modes ($|k| \le 2$), whose regime changes drive the
large-scale circulation; about one test window in five is calm.

\paragraph{Observations.}
Only the upper-layer streamfunction $\psi_1$ is observed, on 3 randomly drawn meridional
columns per day (4.7\,\% of the grid), each once per day, with a 5\,\% observation
error, mimicking along-track satellite altimetry. Scores are computed on 100 test
windows of 30 days, as the mean over the two streamfunction layers $\psi_1, \psi_2$.

\paragraph{Model error.}
At \So{}, the wind given to the DA model has a $+15\,\%$ amplitude bias, a random error of
25\,\% of each mode's standard deviation and a 50\,km position error; the deformation
radius is biased by $-10\,\%$ and the bottom drag by $-50\,\%$; the observation errors
grow to 15\,\% white plus 15\,\% correlated per pass; and the DA model runs on a
$32 \times 32$ grid, which does not resolve the deformation radius.

\paragraph{Data sets.}
The neural schemes are trained on 5000 windows drawn from the same distribution. All are
3D U-Nets over (time, $y$, $x$) trained on 15-day crops, with a random observing system per
draw: 3 to 30 observed (time step, column) slots per day, drawn with a $K^{-1.5}$ tilt towards
sparse configurations, and an observation error between the \Sz{} and \So{} models (5--15\,\%
white, 0--15\,\% correlated per pass). They estimate daily means, interpolated to the 2\,h grid
with cubic splines. D-Unet is applied to the whole 30-day window. CFM is an
observation-conditioned flow (16.8\,M parameters), applied over overlapping 15-day passes, as is
SDA, whose prior is unconditional and never sees observations (10.0\,M parameters; at \So, a
16.8\,M retrain that scores within 0.002 at \Sz). SDA from pure noise follows the published
sampler~\cite{rozet_score-based_2023} ($\gamma = 0.01$, 100 steps, one corrector step,
observation standard deviation at the 5\,\% \Sz{} level); D-Unet+SDA starts the same sampler at
$\tau_0 = 0.5$ from the D-Unet estimate, with 10 steps and a normalised guidance step of length
2, selected on validation windows. Each network is trained with one seed.


\section{Operators and proofs}\label{app:proofs}

\paragraph{Gaussian operator.}
For $q = \mathcal{N}(m, v)$ (per element, standardised units) and $\xv_\tau = \tau
\xv_1 + (1-\tau)\xv_0$, the pair $(\xv_1, \xv_\tau)$ is jointly Gaussian with
$\Cov(\xv_1, \xv_\tau) = \tau v$ and $\mathrm{Var}(\xv_\tau) = \tau^2 v +
(1-\tau)^2$, so
\begin{equation}
  \Psi_q = m + \frac{\tau v}{\tau^2 v + (1-\tau)^2}\,(\xv_\tau - \tau m)
         = m + \frac{\snr\, v}{1 + \snr\, v}\,\Big(\frac{\xv_\tau}{\tau} - m\Big).
\end{equation}
Substituting $\xv_\tau^\star = \tau\xv_1^\star + (1-\tau)\xv_0$ and taking the expectation over $\xv_0$ gives property (iii).

\paragraph{Setting.}
This appendix proves the results of Sects.~\ref{sec:chain} and~\ref{sec:fms} that the analysis relies on: the
budget~\eqref{eq:chain0}--\eqref{eq:residual}, the decomposition~\eqref{eq:fms_div} and the strict propriety of
$\FMS_\tau$, the budget at every $\tau$~\eqref{eq:chaintau} and the restriction
identity~\eqref{eq:restriction_identity}. The truth $\xv_1^\star \in \mathbb{R}^n$ has
$\E\|\xv_1^\star\|^2 < \infty$, and $p(\cdot \mid C)$ is its law given the context. A scheme sees $S = g(C)$, so
that $\sigma(S) \subseteq \sigma(C)$, and reports a law $q(\cdot \mid S, U)$, where $U$ collects its internal
randomness (ensemble draws, perturbed observations, training sample, sampler noise). Its mean is $\hat m$, and
$m_\infty$ is any function of $S$; we write $m = \E[\xv_1^\star \mid C]$ and $m_S = \E[\xv_1^\star \mid S]$. The only
condition on the scheme is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:indep}
  U \text{ is independent of } (\xv_1^\star, C), \qquad
  \xv_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) \text{ is independent of } (\xv_1^\star, C, U).
\end{equation}
It fails, for instance, for an ensemble initialised from perturbations of the true state, or for a network
evaluated on its own training windows. All identities below hold in $[0, \infty]$.

\begin{proposition}[Budget of the mean]\label{prop:budget}
Under~\eqref{eq:indep}, the decomposition~\eqref{eq:chain0} holds. If the misspecification and the approximation
are finite, so does~\eqref{eq:residual}, and the cross term is at most
$2\sqrt{\text{misspecification} \times \text{approximation}}$ in absolute value.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $\hat m - \xv_1^\star = (\hat m - m_S) + (m_S - m) + (m - \xv_1^\star)$. By~\eqref{eq:indep},
$\E[\xv_1^\star \mid C, U] = m$, so $m - \xv_1^\star$ is orthogonal in $L^2$ to every square-integrable function of
$(C, U)$ (the orthogonality principle of the conditional mean), in particular to $m_S - m$ and, when it is
square-integrable, to $\hat m - m_S$. By the tower property and~\eqref{eq:indep},
$\E[m \mid S, U] = \E[m \mid S] = m_S$, so $m - m_S$ is orthogonal to every square-integrable function of
$(S, U)$, in particular to $\hat m - m_S$. Pythagoras gives~\eqref{eq:chain0} when $\E\|\hat m - m_S\|^2 < \infty$;
otherwise both sides are infinite, since $\|\hat m - m_S\| \le \|\hat m - \xv_1^\star\| + \|\xv_1^\star - m_S\|$. The
first term is $\E\|m - \xv_1^\star\|^2 = \E\tr\Cov(\xv_1^\star \mid C)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:residual} expands the
square of $(m_S - m_\infty) + (m_\infty - \hat m)$, and the bound on the cross term is Cauchy--Schwarz.
\end{proof}

Unlike the first two error terms, $m_\infty - \hat m$ need not be orthogonal to the functions of $S$: the
finite-resource error of a scheme can be correlated with its misspecification, which is why the cross term
of~\eqref{eq:residual} remains.

\paragraph{The operator as a Gaussian channel.}
For $\tau \in (0,1)$, $\yv = \xv_\tau / \tau = \xv_1 + \sigma_\tau \xv_0$ with $\sigma_\tau = (1-\tau)/\tau$: an
observation of the whole state in white Gaussian noise, of signal-to-noise ratio
$\snr = \alpha_\tau = \sigma_\tau^{-2} = \tau^2/(1-\tau)^2$. For any probability law $\rho$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$, let
$\varphi_\sigma$ be the $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$ density. The law of $\yv$ has the density
$\rho_\sigma(\yv) = \int \varphi_\sigma(\yv - \xv)\, \rho(d\xv)$, positive and infinitely differentiable on
$\mathbb{R}^n$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psi_explicit}
  \Psi_\rho(\xv_\tau, \tau)
  = \frac{\int \xv\, \varphi_\sigma(\yv - \xv)\, \rho(d\xv)}{\int \varphi_\sigma(\yv - \xv)\, \rho(d\xv)}
  = \yv + \sigma_\tau^2\, \nabla \log \rho_\sigma(\yv), \qquad \yv = \xv_\tau / \tau,\ \sigma = \sigma_\tau ,
\end{equation}
is a version of $\E_\rho[\xv_1 \mid \xv_\tau]$, finite and continuous at every $\yv$ with no moment assumption on
$\rho$, since $\|\xv\|\varphi_\sigma(\yv - \xv)$ is bounded in $\xv$. The second form, Tweedie's formula, follows by
differentiating $\rho_\sigma$ under the integral; with $\nabla_{\xv_\tau} = \tau^{-1}\nabla_{\yv}$ it is the form of
Sect.~\ref{sec:fms}, and it gives the Fisher form~\eqref{eq:fisher}. Two properties follow at once. At $\tau = 0$,
$\xv_\tau = \xv_0$ is independent of $\xv_1$ and $\Psi_q = \mathbf{m}_q$: property~(i). A point estimate $\hat m$
is the law $\delta_{\hat m}$, for which~\eqref{eq:psi_explicit} gives $\Psi_q \equiv \hat m$ at every $\tau$:
property~(iv). The context enters as a parameter: $\Psi_p(\xv_\tau, C, \tau)$ is~\eqref{eq:psi_explicit} with
$\rho = p(\cdot \mid C)$. Since $\xv_0$ is independent of $(\xv_1^\star, C)$,
$\Psi_p = \E[\xv_1^\star \mid \xv_\tau, C]$ and $\Psi_{p_S} = \E[\xv_1^\star \mid \xv_\tau, S]$, while $\Psi_q$ is a
function of $(\xv_\tau, S, U)$.

\begin{proposition}[Decomposition and strict propriety]\label{prop:proper}
For $\tau \in (0,1)$, $\FMS_\tau(q) = D_\tau(p \,\|\, q) + \mathrm{mmse}_p(\tau)$ with
$\mathrm{mmse}_p(\tau) \le n/\alpha_\tau$, and $\FMS_\tau$ is strictly proper: its expectation over contexts is
minimised by $q(\cdot \mid C) = p(\cdot \mid C)$, and only by laws equal to $p(\cdot \mid C)$ for almost every $C$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The estimator $\yv$ has error $\sigma_\tau \xv_0$, so $\mathrm{mmse}_p(\tau) \le n\sigma_\tau^2 = n/\alpha_\tau$.
Since $\Psi_p = \E[\xv_1^\star \mid \xv_\tau, C]$, $\xv_1^\star - \Psi_p$ is orthogonal to every square-integrable
function of $(\xv_\tau, C)$, and the argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:budget} gives the decomposition. The
term $\mathrm{mmse}_p(\tau)$ does not depend on $q$, so the expected score of $q$ exceeds that of $p$ by
$D_\tau \ge 0$. Suppose $D_\tau = 0$. For almost every $C$, $\Psi_q = \Psi_p$ almost everywhere under the law of
$\yv$, whose density $p_\sigma(\cdot \mid C)$ is positive; the two operators thus agree Lebesgue-almost everywhere,
hence everywhere by continuity. By~\eqref{eq:psi_explicit}, $\nabla \log p_\sigma = \nabla \log q_\sigma$ on
$\mathbb{R}^n$, so $p_\sigma = c\, q_\sigma$, and $c = 1$ since both integrate to one. The Fourier transform of a
Gaussian convolution is a product, $\hat p(\boldsymbol{\omega})\, e^{-\sigma_\tau^2\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|^2/2}
= \hat q(\boldsymbol{\omega})\, e^{-\sigma_\tau^2\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|^2/2}$, so the characteristic functions,
hence the laws, coincide: Gaussian smoothing is injective. With internal randomness, the argument applies to
$q(\cdot \mid S, U)$ for almost every $U$, by~\eqref{eq:indep}.
\end{proof}

For a one-dimensional slice $\mathbf{u}^\top \xv_1$, the proof applies to the law of the slice. If the directions are
drawn with a positive density on the sphere, a zero expected divergence forces equality of the slice laws for almost
every direction, hence, by continuity of the characteristic functions along directions, for every direction, and of
the joint laws by the Cramér--Wold theorem: property~(ii) for the sliced score.

\begin{proposition}[Budget at every $\tau$]\label{prop:chaintau}
Under~\eqref{eq:indep} and for $\tau \in (0,1)$, the decomposition~\eqref{eq:chaintau} holds, with
$D_\tau(p \,\|\, p_S) = \E\|\Psi_p - \Psi_{p_S}\|^2$ and $D_\tau(p_S \,\|\, q) = \E\|\Psi_{p_S} - \Psi_q\|^2$,
expectations under the true law, and its residual splits as~\eqref{eq:residual} with the operators in place of the
means. The irreducible and restriction terms are both at most $n/\alpha_\tau$, and vanish as $\tau \to 1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:budget} to the augmented contexts $C_\tau = (C, \xv_\tau)$ and $S_\tau = (S, \xv_\tau)$,
with $\sigma(S_\tau) \subseteq \sigma(C_\tau)$, and to the estimator $\Psi_q$, a function of $(S_\tau, U)$. Condition~\eqref{eq:indep}
carries over: $U$ is independent of $(\xv_1^\star, C, \xv_0)$, hence of $(\xv_1^\star, C_\tau)$. The conditional means
are $\Psi_p$ and $\Psi_{p_S}$, and no moment assumption is needed, since
$\mathrm{mmse}_p(\tau) \le \mathrm{mmse}_{p_S}(\tau) \le n/\alpha_\tau$ and, by Pythagoras, the restriction is
$\mathrm{mmse}_{p_S}(\tau) - \mathrm{mmse}_p(\tau) \le n/\alpha_\tau$.
\end{proof}

The integrals over the signal-to-noise ratio connect these terms to information measures. For laws with finite
second moments, $\tfrac12\int_0^\infty D_\tau(p_S \,\|\, q)\, d\alpha_\tau = \E\,\mathrm{KL}(p_S \,\|\, q)$, the
mismatched-estimation identity of~\cite{verdu_mismatched_2010}, and the restriction integrates to
$2\, I(\xv_1^\star; C \mid S)$ by the I-MMSE relation of~\cite{guo_mutual_2005} applied conditionally on $S$; the
irreducible term alone is not integrable when $\xv_1^\star$ has a density. These integrals are not used in the analysis.

\begin{proposition}[Restriction identity]\label{prop:restriction}
If two schemes report the exact posteriors given $S$ and $S' = g'(S)$, i.e.\ $\mathbf{m}_q = m_S$,
$\Cov_q = \Cov(\xv_1^\star \mid S)$ and likewise for $q'$ and $S'$, then~\eqref{eq:restriction_identity} holds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:budget}, with $S$ as the full context, $S'$ as the restriction and the exact scheme $q'$, gives
$\E\|m_{S'} - \xv_1^\star\|^2 = \E\tr\Cov(\xv_1^\star \mid S) + \E\|m_S - m_{S'}\|^2$, and
$\E\|m_S - \xv_1^\star\|^2 = \E\tr\Cov(\xv_1^\star \mid S)$, $\E\|m_{S'} - \xv_1^\star\|^2 = \E\tr\Cov(\xv_1^\star \mid S')$.
\end{proof}
For actual schemes, the two sides of~\eqref{eq:restriction_identity} are computed from their reported means and
variances; a gap between them measures their departure from the exact posteriors, i.e.\ misspecification or
approximation.

\paragraph{Mixture operator.}
For a Gaussian mixture $q = \sum_i w_i\, \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{m}_i, \Sigma_i)$, conditioning on the component gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psi_mix}
\begin{gathered}
  \Psi_q = \sum_i \pi_i(\xv_\tau)\, \Psi_i(\xv_\tau), \qquad
  \pi_i \propto w_i\, \mathcal{N}\big(\xv_\tau;\, \tau \mathbf{m}_i,\, \tau^2 \Sigma_i + (1-\tau)^2 I\big),\\
  \Psi_i = \mathbf{m}_i + \tau \Sigma_i \big(\tau^2 \Sigma_i + (1-\tau)^2 I\big)^{-1}(\xv_\tau - \tau \mathbf{m}_i),
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
the posterior-weighted average of the Gaussian operators. A kernel-density estimate is the case
$\Sigma_i = h^2 I$, $w_i = 1/N$. An ensemble $\{\xv_1^{(i)}\}$ is the case $\Sigma_i = 0$: $\Psi_i = \xv_1^{(i)}$ and
$\pi_i \propto \exp(-\alpha_\tau \|\yv - \xv_1^{(i)}\|^2/2)$: $\Psi_q$ is a softmax-weighted average of the members. As $\tau \to 1$, these
weights concentrate on the member nearest to $\yv \to \xv_1^\star$, and the score of the atomic form tends to
$\E\min_i \|\xv_1^{(i)} - \xv_1^\star\|^2$ (nearest-member snapping), which does not vanish for a finite ensemble
drawn from the true posterior: at large $\tau$, the atomic score reads the sampling density of the ensemble as well
as its law. Hence the kernel and per-element forms used in practice (Appendix~\ref{app:fms}).
